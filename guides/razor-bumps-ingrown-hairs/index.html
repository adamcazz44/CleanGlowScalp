Scalp care

Razor bumps and ingrown hairs on the scalp

The little red bumps after a shave aren't bad luck — they're hairs curling back into your skin. Here's how to shave so you get far fewer, how to calm the ones you've got, and the kind of bump that's worth a doctor's look.

Updated July 20, 2026

Nicks I expected. The bumps took me longer to figure out — especially at the back, where I can't see what I'm doing. Turns out most of it comes down to how close I was shaving and which direction I was going.

What's actually happening

When you cut a hair short, the tip can curl back and grow into the skin instead of straight out. Your body treats that ingrown hair like an invader, and the inflamed result is a razor bump.[source] The closer the shave, the more likely the hair retracts below the surface and curves back in — and coarse or curly hair is the most prone to it.

Worth taking seriously: left alone, repeated razor bumps can leave permanent marks — deep grooves and raised scars.[source] The good news is that prevention is mostly about how you shave, not luck.

How to shave so you get fewer

These are the habits dermatologists point to:

  • Soften first. Shave at the end of a shower, or hold a warm, damp cloth on your scalp for a few minutes. Warm, wet hair swells and is less likely to curl back into the skin.[source]
  • Shave with the grain. Going against it shaves closer but irritates more. Learn which way the hair grows across your scalp and follow it.
  • Don't chase the closest shave. Don't stretch the skin taut, and ease off the baby-smooth finish — that's what drives hairs back under the surface. If multi-blade razors are leaving you bumpy, a single- or two-blade razor (or an electric one that leaves a touch of stubble) can help.
  • Use the right products. A non-comedogenic cleanser first, a moisturizing shave cream or gel during (let it sit a minute and keep it wet), and a soothing, alcohol-free aftershave balm after.[source]
  • Exfoliate gently, a couple times a week. A mild chemical exfoliant (salicylic or glycolic acid) clears the dead skin that traps hairs. Skip harsh gritty scrubs, and skip it entirely while skin is irritated.
  • Keep the blade sharp. A dull blade tugs and greatly raises the risk of bumps. Replace a disposable blade every 5–7 shaves, or clean an electric shaver on the same schedule, and store it dry.[source]

Calming the bumps you already have

The most effective thing is also the least fun: stop shaving for a bit so the trapped hairs can grow out. Expect to keep seeing some bumps at first as short hairs surface — they usually thin out within a month and clear within about three.[source] In the meantime: a warm compress to loosen trapped hairs, gentle exfoliation once the skin has calmed, a soothing balm, and hands off — no picking or digging, which only invites infection and scarring.

When a bump needs a dermatologist

See a board-certified dermatologist if:

  • bumps keep coming back after you've fixed your shaving habits;[source]
  • a bump becomes painful, swollen, or pus-filled — that can mean infection;
  • you're getting scars or dark marks where the bumps were.

One that's specific to us: firm, itchy bumps low on the back of the scalp or neck that look like razor bumps can be a condition called acne keloidalis nuchae. Caught early by a dermatologist it's manageable; left alone it can lead to raised scars and permanent hair loss, so it's worth a check rather than a wait.[source] Where we draw the line between a tip and a doctor's job is on our not-medical-advice page. Common questions

Why do I get razor bumps on my head?

When you cut a hair short, the tip can curl back and grow into the skin instead of out of it. Your body treats that ingrown hair as an irritant and you get an inflamed bump. The closer the shave, the more likely the hair retracts and curls back in — and coarse or curly hair is more prone to it.

Should I shave my head with or against the grain?

With the grain. Shaving against the grain gives a closer cut but causes more irritation and more bumps. Figure out which way the hair grows on different parts of your scalp and follow it — it's a slightly less close shave, which is exactly the point.

How do I get rid of razor bumps fast?

There's no instant fix. The most reliable move is to stop shaving for a while so trapped hairs can grow out — bumps usually thin out within a month and clear within about three. A warm compress, gentle exfoliation once the skin calms, and a soothing aftershave help. Don't pick at them.

Cream, gel, or conditioner to shave my head with?

Use a proper moisturizing shave cream or gel — it's built to soften hair and let the blade glide, which is what prevents bumps. Hair conditioner works as a slick backup in a pinch, but it isn't formulated for shaving. What matters most is that your skin is wet and the lubricant stays wet while you shave.
