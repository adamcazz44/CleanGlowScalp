The best electric head shavers for bald men

The tool is the foundation of the whole routine. Here's how to choose between rotary and foil, what actually matters, and the models that come up again and again — from budget to premium.

Get the shaver right and the daily shave goes from a chore to two minutes. Get it wrong and you fight nicks and missed patches every morning. This is what to look for — and the models worth a look.

How to choose (this matters more than the brand)

Before any specific model, get these four calls right:

  • Rotary vs foil. Rotary shavers have round, floating heads that follow the curves of a scalp — the right default for a head. Foil shavers are gentler and better suited to sensitive skin or razor-bump-prone scalps, but they're built for faces and don't hug a head as naturally.
  • Number of heads. Four to five heads is the sweet spot — enough coverage per pass without getting bulky and hard to control around the ears and neckline. More than that is mostly marketing.[source]
  • Waterproofing. If you shave in the shower, you want full water resistance (IPX7, rated for submersion). A splash rating (IPX6) is fine if you shave dry or at the sink.[source]
  • Running cost. Don't just look at the sticker price — replacement blades or heads vary a lot between brands, and that adds up over the years. A cheap shaver with pricey heads can cost more long-term.

Common questions

Rotary or foil for a bald head?

Rotary for most people. Its round, floating heads follow the curves of your scalp, which is exactly what you need up top. Foil shavers are gentler and a good shout if you have sensitive skin or get razor bumps, but they're really designed for faces and don't hug a head as naturally.

How much should I spend on a head shaver?

You don't need to go premium. Solid dedicated head shavers start in the budget tier and do the core job well. Paying more mostly buys battery life, waterproofing, accessories, and brand support — nice to have, not essential. What matters more is matching the shaver to your skin and routine.

Are the expensive ones worth it?

Sometimes. A premium foil shaver can be worth it if you have sensitive skin or bad razor bumps. Otherwise, the jump from a good mid-range rotary to a premium one is usually about convenience and accessories, not a dramatically better shave.

How often do I replace the blades?

Follow the maker's guidance — typically every several months to a year, depending on the model. Running cost matters here: replacement heads vary a lot between brands, so factor that in, not just the sticker price.
